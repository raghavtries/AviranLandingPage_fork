From new client to integrated,
without a new hire.

  1. Understand

    Aviran learns your agent once: what changes per client, from platforms and prompts to tools and policies. That becomes a product contract every later client reuses.

  2. Onboard

    For each new client, Aviran turns their docs, calls, and recordings into cited requirements and business logic. It prepares the config and platform mappings, including a platform you haven’t integrated before.

  3. Maintain

    After launch, clients describe the change they want. Aviran finds what needs updating and proposes a diff with a rollback path. Nothing applies until your FDE approves it.

What carries over from client to client.

Product contract, once
Aviran learns your configurable objects, workflows, and permission boundaries one time. Every later client starts from that contract, so each rollout takes less custom work than the last.
Repeat work, flagged
As you onboard more clients, Aviran flags the work that keeps coming back, so you can tell what belongs in your core product and what stays custom.
Owner-routed blockers
Most implementation delay sits with the client: missing access, unclear ownership, unanswered questions. Aviran finds who owns each blocker, asks them directly, and logs the answer as an implementation record.
Approve, don’t audit
Every generated config and every post-launch change ships with a diff, validation evidence, and a rollback path. Nothing goes live without operator approval.

The same loop, stage by stage.

Understand, onboard, and maintain, broken into the five stages you see in the product. Each stage leaves an artifact you can read, and nothing ships without your approval.

Learn the product once.

Aviran inspects your documentation, schemas, APIs, repositories, and sample deployments. The result is a confirmed product contract: what’s configurable, what’s supported, and what needs engineering.

  • Identifies configurable prompts, policies, tools, and workflows
  • Maps supported integrations, field mappings, and permissions
  • Skippable when you already hand us a complete contract

Extract requirements, not assumptions.

Aviran reads client documents, call transcripts, screen recordings, policies, Slack threads, and sample data. It extracts structured, cited requirements and routes open questions to the right person.

  • Every requirement is traceable to its source
  • Missing or conflicting information is flagged and routed automatically
  • Blocker owners and status are tracked until resolved

Generate the customer-specific build.

Aviran combines your product contract with the client’s confirmed requirements. The result includes configuration, field mappings, workflows, and acceptance criteria for testing. When the client uses a platform you haven’t integrated before, Aviran helps wire that integration too.

  • Config, mappings, and workflows generated from the product contract
  • Acceptance tests and an implementation plan produced alongside the build
  • Every artifact traces back to a source requirement

Prove it works before it ships.

Aviran checks the proposed configuration against your product contract for schema compatibility, permission correctness, expected behavior, and regressions. Each check produces evidence beyond a simple pass or fail.

  • Contract compliance and schema checks
  • Behavioral validation against acceptance criteria
  • Failed checks include proposed remediation

Approve once. Maintain forever.

First, Aviran presents the plan, risks, and rollback path for operator approval. Then Aviran launches the customer. After launch, Aviran suggests supported changes for approval before they ship. Examples include policy updates, field mappings, and routing rules.

  • Full plan, risk, and rollback surfaced before launch
  • Post-launch changes are suggested, then approved, never silent
  • Unsupported work escalates to engineering with full context

Common questions.

Why wouldn’t we build this internally?

Client onboarding is a different surface from your core product. Doing it well takes requirements intake, config generation, validation, approvals, rollback, and an audit trail. Building that in-house pulls engineers off the product, and doing it by hand means hiring another FDE every few clients.

What can Aviran actually change without approval?

Nothing. Aviran suggests changes such as policy updates, prompt and workflow edits, field mappings, integration config, and routing rules, and shows the exact diff for each one. You approve every change before it goes live.

What happens when the customer is the blocker?

Aviran finds who owns each blocker, asks them directly, and turns the answer into an implementation record, for example: “Salesforce sandbox access (Owner: Acme CRM admin, Status: Waiting).”

What if a client runs on a system with no API?

If a client runs on a proprietary ERP or EHR, Aviran can’t generate endpoints for your agent to call yet. It still maps how the client works in that system, so your FDEs know what the integration needs before they build it.
